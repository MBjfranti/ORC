# 09 — MIDI Implementation

The MIDI design is the most quietly sophisticated thing about the Orchid and the part most
worth copying wholesale.

## The three-channel split **[OFFICIAL]**

Orchid transmits **three simultaneous, semantically distinct MIDI streams**:

| Channel | Stream | Contents |
|---------|--------|----------|
| **1** | **Performance** | The final notes you actually hear — including arpeggios, strums, harp cascades, and **melodies generated by turning the Voicing dial** |
| **2** | **Bass** | The dedicated bass synth's single notes |
| **3** | **Chord** | The raw, sustained block chord — *regardless of performance mode* |

> "Orchid is a powerful MIDI controller because it outputs its performance, bass, and chord data
> on three separate, simultaneous MIDI channels. This allows for incredible flexibility in your DAW."

Why this is smart: **Channel 3 is the harmony as data; Channel 1 is the harmony as gesture.**
Record both and you can re-perform the same progression with a different articulation later, or
feed the block chords to a pad while the arpeggio drives a pluck. Channel 2 keeps the bass on
its own track for independent instrument assignment.

### Configuration **[OFFICIAL]**

`Options → Audio and MIDI → MIDI Channels` — each of the three streams can be **assigned to any
channel (1–16)** or set to **Off**.

**v3.90 change:** *"MIDI channels now have an Off setting"* and *"**Chord MIDI channel now
defaults to Off**"* — presumably because the duplicate-notes problem (below) bit enough users.

**[PRESS]** Marketing describes routing chords, arps and basslines "across separate MIDI
channels. Up to 16 of them" and driving "large multi-synth setups via MIDI routing and thru."

## Physical MIDI **[OFFICIAL]**

### USB-C
- Carries **MIDI and power only — no audio.**
- Requires a **data-capable** USB-C cable; charge-only cables silently fail.
- On boot with USB attached, a **"Select USB Mode"** menu appears — use the Options dial to
  select **"Enable USB MIDI."**
- Boot sequence matters: power the Orchid off, plug in USB, power on, and **don't touch any
  keys/dials during the boot animation.**
- macOS Ventura+ on Apple Silicon may treat the Orchid as a power supply. Fix:
  System Settings → Privacy & Security → "Allow accessories to connect" → "Ask for new
  accessories" → Allow when prompted.

### 5-pin DIN MIDI Out
- Standard **5-pin DIN**, not 3.5 mm TRS.
- Connecting to TRS-MIDI gear requires the correct **Type A vs Type B** adapter — these swap
  tip/ring and are mutually incompatible. Arturia gear (e.g. MicroFreak) commonly uses Type B.
  A switchable adapter (Retrokits RK-TRS-X) solves it. **[OFFICIAL/COMMUNITY]**
- Supports MIDI thru chaining for multi-synth rigs. **[PRESS]**

### Connecting to other USB MIDI devices **[OFFICIAL]**
Orchid is a USB **device**, not a **host** — you cannot plug it directly into another USB MIDI
device (OP-1 Field, etc.). You need a **USB MIDI host box**. Community-recommended:
**Intech Studio Knot**, **Doremidi DUH-10**.

## MIDI In — the undocumented feature **[COMMUNITY, confirmed in official docs]**

> "While not an officially documented feature yet, the community discovered that Orchid can
> **receive** MIDI notes via its USB-C port, allowing you to play its internal synth engine with
> an external controller."

The important part:

> "**Polyphony:** while Orchid's own keyboard is monophonic (for single notes), the synth engine
> responds **polyphonically** to incoming MIDI data. This means you can play your own complex
> chords from a larger keyboard and hear them with Orchid's unique sounds."

**Limitations:** MIDI In triggers only the raw synth engine (Ch 1) and the bass engine (Ch 2).
It does **not** engage chord generation or performance modes. So it's a sound module, not a
remote control for the chord logic.

This tells us something structurally useful: **the chord engine and the synth engine are
cleanly separated inside the device**, and the chord engine sits *upstream* of MIDI-in. Our
architecture should mirror that — chord logic → performance layer → (synth engine | MIDI out),
with an input path that can bypass the first two stages.

## Known issues

### Duplicate notes in FL Studio **[OFFICIAL]**
FL Studio routes MIDI from unassigned channels to the currently selected channel-rack
instrument. Since Orchid always sends on 1, 2 and 3, you get duplicated notes.

Workarounds: the **"MIDI trash bin"** method (create two dummy instruments locked to the unwanted
channels), or simply set the unwanted channels to **Off** on the Orchid. Telepathic state they're
"actively working on fixing this in future firmware updates."

### Duplicate note-offs **[OFFICIAL]**
v3.90 fixed: *"Chord MIDI channel no longer duplicates note-off messages."* Worth remembering as
a class of bug when we write our own MIDI out — note-off hygiene is where these things break.

### Ground loop noise **[OFFICIAL]**
A long support article covers high-pitched whine when USB-C and the 3.5 mm output are used
simultaneously. Not our problem, but it explains why so many users work over MIDI-into-DAW
rather than recording the Orchid's audio.

### Audio recording **[OFFICIAL]**
The 3.5 mm output is **stereo TRS**. Summing to a single mono input causes phasing and the
"muddy/distorted" recordings users report. Correct approach: 3.5 mm TRS → dual 1/4" TS breakout
into two interface inputs, recorded to a stereo track.

## Clock **[UNKNOWN / PRESS]**

At least one review states there is **no MIDI clock**. Not confirmed or contradicted by the
official docs, and firmware may have changed it. Do not treat as settled.

## DAW setup summary **[OFFICIAL]**

Telepathic publish per-DAW guides for **Logic Pro, Ableton Live, Pro Tools and FL Studio**. The
common shape in all four:

1. Enable Orchid as a MIDI input device in preferences.
2. Create **three** MIDI/instrument tracks.
3. Set each track's MIDI input to **one specific channel** — never "All"/Omni, which produces
   overlapping, muddy duplicate notes.
4. Arm all three and record.

The repeated warning across every guide — *don't record all channels to one track* — is the
clearest signal that the three-stream design is central to how the instrument is meant to be used.

## Implications for our build **[DERIVED]**

- **Implement the three-stream split.** Web MIDI (`navigator.requestMIDIAccess`) supports
  multiple channels on one output port trivially. This is high value, low cost, and it's the
  feature that makes the thing useful to actual producers.
- **Make channel assignment configurable, including Off**, and default the Chord channel to Off
  exactly as v3.90 does — same reasoning applies to us.
- **Accept MIDI In** for polyphonic playing of our sound engine, *and* consider going further
  than the hardware: let incoming MIDI notes drive the **chord engine** (a single incoming note
  = a root note). The Orchid can't do this; we easily can, and it makes any MIDI controller a
  full Orchid keyboard.
- **Be rigorous about note-offs** — track every sounding note per stream and flush on
  panic/blur/mode-change. Browsers lose focus constantly; stuck notes will be our #1 bug.
